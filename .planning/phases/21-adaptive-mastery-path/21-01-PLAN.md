Write these TWENTY-TWO, verbatim in intent (wording may be tightened, meaning may
not change). Each is derived from a locked decision in `21-CONTEXT.md`:

- **REQ-103** — A single cross-type competency catalog exists, derived
  mechanically from the engine's rubric dimensions (the four shared keys plus
  every type's `extraRubricDimensions`), with a TOTAL dimension-to-competency
  mapping: every dimension key in the registry maps to exactly one competency,
  and a type shipping an unmapped dimension fails a verifier. The catalog is
  structured so a later faculty-authored framework replaces it without reworking
  consumers.
- **REQ-104** — Mastery is computed by a pure deterministic function: per
  competency, a LIFETIME figure over every scored contribution and a
  CURRENT-WINDOW figure over a bounded recent window, plus one overall figure.
  No model call participates in the arithmetic.
- **REQ-105** — Every mastery figure carries its evidence count and an explicit
  `thin` flag at a declared threshold. A thin figure is SHOWN with its thinness
  flagged — never withheld, and never presented as if well-evidenced.
- **REQ-106** — The student sees an overall mastery percentage plus a
  per-competency breakdown, with the lifetime and current-window figures
  labelled so the distinction is unmistakable, and thinness visible. The surface
  is REACHABLE from the authenticated student navigation — a page a student can
  only find by typing a URL does not satisfy this.
- **REQ-107** — Both live sessions and knowledge-check completions move mastery
  through one shared contribution ledger, with a declared weight per source.
  Stated as the student-observable condition it has to be: **a completed
  practice session whose report reaches READY writes `LIVE_SESSION`
  contributions for every competency that report scored**, so a student who has
  done sessions and taken no checks still sees non-empty mastery. "Mastery stays
  empty after normal practice" is a REQ-107 failure, not a missing feature.
  The ORDERING is enforced mechanically: deterministically-graded checks outrank
  model-graded rewrites, and unaided practice outranks coached practice.
- **REQ-108** — All four knowledge-check formats (multiple choice, fill in the
  blank, pick the better response, rewrite a weak line) are modelled, persisted
  INSIDE the study-plan surface, **and ANSWERABLE BY THE STUDENT**: an
  authenticated surface renders each of the four formats and submits an answer
  through an authenticated, ownership-scoped route. A modelled format with no way
  to answer it does not satisfy this. A knowledge check is NOT a registered
  engine interaction type — a surface-count guard fails if a knowledge-check
  slug reaches the engine registry.
- **REQ-109** — Check content is generated from the student's OWN session
  moments — real transcript turns and the growth areas their report identified —
  under a named, scoped widening of the study-plan privacy boundary that is
  recorded with its rationale. The existing closed-vocabulary generator path is
  unchanged.
- **REQ-110** — A generated question that quotes the student reproduces a stored
  transcript turn EXACTLY, carrying its originating report id and turn index. The
  match is validated mechanically against the stored transcript at generation
  time; a non-matching quote is REJECTED, never repaired or paraphrased.
- **REQ-111** — When a student submits an answer through REQ-108's surface, the
  three closed formats are graded deterministically and the student is shown
  their mark — with the answer key never reaching the browser in any response.
  Model grading is used only for rewrites, and only at the lower weight REQ-107
  declares.
- **REQ-112** — A student can contest a grading **from the surface that showed
  them the mark**, without leaving the app. A contest is recorded, reverses that
  item's mastery contribution pending review, and flags the generated question —
  so a bad generated question surfaces instead of quietly damaging mastery. A
  contest endpoint with no affordance does not satisfy this.
- **REQ-113** — Coached practice is its own instance that a student can actually
  START, and START ONLY from a step in a study plan's path. Both halves are
  conditions: a path-step entry point EXISTS and works, and no normal practice
  entry point can launch it, proven by a negative guard. Its relationship to the
  engine is decided and recorded explicitly rather than left implicit.
- **REQ-114** — A student can COMPLETE a coached session, and in completing it
  experiences REPLAY FIRST, THEN FRESH: the student is shown the actual
  transcript moment to rework and works it, and only then is presented a new
  situation exercising the same competency, which the session cannot finish
  without. A runtime that stores the two legs but never presents them does not
  satisfy this.
- **REQ-115** — Coach tips are TECHNIQUE REMINDERS ONLY. A tip names the skill
  and never scripts the student's words. Enforced by a mechanical check on tip
  content, not by prompt instruction alone — and the check runs on EVERY tip
  actually rendered to a student during a session, not only on tips at authoring
  time. A validator with no delivery path to guard does not satisfy this.
- **REQ-116** — Completing a coached session produces a scored contribution that
  moves the student's VISIBLE mastery figure at the coached weight, below unaided
  practice — the student can complete a coached session and then see the figure
  change on the mastery surface.
- **REQ-117** — A path is a CHILD of a study plan, bounded at 5-10 steps. A new
  study plan generates a NEW path; old paths are neither discarded nor carried
  forward and stay reachable through the study plan that owns them.
- **REQ-118** — "What's next" is deterministic and explainable: step selection
  targets the weakest competency first and returns the reason it chose that step.
- **REQ-119** — Per-competency levels with declared thresholds and daily/weekly
  goals with declared defaults exist. Streaks and unlocking exist NOWHERE in the
  surface, proven by a negative guard.
- **REQ-120** — The path is surfaced with step state, next-step reason, level and
  goal progress, and the existing study-plan surface on `app/reports/page.tsx`
  keeps working unchanged, regression-guarded.
- **REQ-122** — Every path step renders an action that reaches the exercise it
  names: a `KNOWLEDGE_CHECK` step leads to an answerable check, a
  `COACHED_SESSION` step leads to a startable coached session, an
  `UNAIDED_SESSION` step leads to normal practice. A step whose action link goes
  nowhere is a REQ-122 failure.
- **REQ-123** — Check generation's inputs — session moments, the user id owning
  each moment, the transcripts they came from, and the student's own display
  names — are assembled by ONE named, exported, ownership-scoped assembler that
  is testable without a route. The privacy contract REQ-109 states is satisfied
  by construction rather than by whatever a route improvises.
- **REQ-124** — The mapping from a report's scored rubric dimensions UP into
  competency contributions exists in exactly ONE module, used by BOTH live-session
  and coached-session ingestion. Two copies of that mapping is a REQ-124 failure,
  because they will diverge and the two sources will stop being comparable.
- **REQ-121** — REQ-103..REQ-120 and REQ-122..REQ-124 are recorded in
  REQUIREMENTS.md and ROADMAP.md,
  and the REQ-85 amendment is recorded as a Phase 18 revision handoff section in
  Phase 21's OWN validation record — `18-VALIDATION.md` is not edited.
