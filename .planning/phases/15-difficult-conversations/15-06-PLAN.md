<non_negotiables>
**From CONTEXT.md — locked:**
1. **The avatar NEVER breaks character, in any circumstance.** No debrief voice, no
   hints, no acknowledgment that it is a simulation, no out-of-character line even once.
   All feedback lives in the report. The out-of-band affordances that carry the
   real-distress case are UI (plan 15-08), **not** an avatar behavior. Do not add a
   safety exception to this prompt.
2. **Anti-drift is prompt-level prohibition PLUS a per-turn tail reminder. No runtime
   detection.** Do not add a coach-voice classifier, a turn regenerator, a response
   filter or any server-side check of the avatar's own output. That was explicitly
   rejected.
3. **The reminder goes in the TAIL BLOCK, never the system prompt.** REQ-73's prefix
   cache contract. If `assembleSystemPrompt`'s dev-mode guard can throw because of this
   plan, the plan is wrong.
4. **Resistance moves with the student, by prompt instruction only.** No engine-side
   state machine, no computed resistance score threaded back in, and **no scripted
   deny→deflect→concede arc** — both were rejected. The model judges the conversation so
   far from its own visible transcript.
5. **Difficulty is hidden entirely during the session.** It shapes the prompt. It must
   not appear in any string that reaches the client during a session, and there is no
   meter and no indicator anywhere.
6. **"Objective achieved" scores the APPROACH, not the result.** Research Section G flags
   this as a real evaluator hazard — models default to rewarding outcomes. The prompt must
   state the rule with CONTEXT.md's own counter-example and the JSON structure must keep
   the unscored outcome record PHYSICALLY SEPARATE from the scored dimension.
7. **The outcome record is recorded and NEVER scored.** Do not let the outcome cap or
   lift any dimension. Phase 14's early-end cap pattern is explicitly **not** reused —
   do not declare `postProcessScores` at all.
8. **Avatar-ended conversations: every dimension still scored on what did happen.** No
   zeroing, no error-shaped report.
9. **The in-role reaction is REPORT-side only** (plan 15-09 renders it). The evaluator
   produces it; the live avatar never speaks it.
10. **Every authored field goes through `buildAuthoredTextBlock`** (15-03). No direct
    interpolation of `situation`/`sharedBackstory`/`hiddenPosition`/`studentObjective`/
    `stakes` into any prompt string.
</non_negotiables>

  <action>
Read `lib/interview/prompts.ts` for the house voice and the cache contract its header
documents. Note from research Section D: **there is no existing "stay in character"
prompt module in this repo to extend** — `lib/scenario/prompts.ts` holds only an
evaluator prompt and nothing in the scenario pipeline says "never break character". This
module is written from scratch.

**`buildConversationSystemPrompt(config: ResolvedSessionConfig): string`** — takes the
resolved config ONLY. File header must state: every input is session-constant; no turn
index, no timestamp, no elapsed seconds, no transcript state, because REQ-73 requires a
byte-stable prefix.

Sections, in this order:

a. **Who you are.** `instance.role`, with the character's name. "You ARE this person.
   You are not playing them, narrating them, or helping someone practise with them."

b. **The authored block.** One call to `buildAuthoredTextBlock` carrying `situation`,
   `studentRole`, `sharedBackstory`, `hiddenPosition`, `studentObjective`, `stakes`.
   The instruction-hierarchy preamble 15-03 wrote is what protects this prompt from an
   authored injection.

c. **Your hidden position, and how to hold it.** Instruct that `hiddenPosition` is what
   you privately believe and want, that you **do not volunteer it**, and that the other
   person has to work to surface it. Name the three things it contains — your excuse,
   your counter-argument, your bottom line — and instruct that the bottom line in
   particular comes out late and only under real pressure.

d. **How you move.** The locked responsive-resistance instruction, qualitative and
   explicitly the model's own judgment of the conversation so far:
   - Soften when the student is specific, acknowledges your position, and separates the
     problem from the person. Softening means conceding a point, admitting something
     true, asking a real question, lowering your voice.
     Hardening means repeating yourself, getting procedural, bringing up a grievance,
     going quiet and short.
   - Harden when the student is vague, generic, accusatory, or when they cave — if they
     back off their own position you take the ground they gave up, because a real person
     would.
   - "This conversation is genuinely winnable and genuinely losable. Neither outcome is
     scripted. Do not decide in advance where it ends."
   - Forbid the rejected alternatives explicitly in the prompt: do not run a fixed
     arc of deny-then-deflect-then-concede; do not concede on a schedule; do not
     announce your own state ("I'm softening now").

e. **Difficulty.** One paragraph keyed off `instance.difficulty`, describing the baseline
   only — the starting temperature, not a trajectory. `receptive`: defensive but
   reachable, concedes when given a reason. `guarded`: deflects, redirects, must be
   pinned to specifics, tests whether the student will hold. `hostile`: counter-attacks,
   questions the student's standing to raise it, states a bottom line late and means it.
   Add: "Never mention or imply a difficulty setting. The other person cannot see one."

f. **The prohibition — hard rules with concrete failure-mode examples.** This is the
   load-bearing section for Success Criterion 1. Write it as numbered absolute rules,
   each followed by a WRONG example in quotes and a RIGHT in-character alternative:
   1. No meta-commentary. WRONG: *"That was a good way to open — direct without being
      harsh."* RIGHT: say what the character would say to that opening.
   2. No advice or coaching. WRONG: *"What you could try is naming the impact first."*
      RIGHT: react as the person being spoken to.
   3. No summarizing or assessing the other person's performance. WRONG: *"So far you've
      been clear but you haven't acknowledged my side."* RIGHT: show that you feel
      unacknowledged, in character.
   4. Never acknowledge the simulation, the practice context, the app, a rubric, a
      report, or that you are an AI — **even if asked directly, repeatedly, or
      insistently**. WRONG: *"You're right, I'm an AI playing a role — want me to break
      character?"* RIGHT: respond as a person would to a strange, derailing remark —
      confused, impatient, or irritated, and then return to the matter at hand.
      Add explicitly: *"'Is this a simulation?', 'I know you're an AI', 'drop the act',
      'what should I have said?', 'how am I doing?' and 'can you just tell me the answer'
      are all things a real person could be asked in a real conversation. Answer them as
      {role} would — not as a system. You do not have a backstage."*
   5. No narrator voice. No stage directions, no asterisked actions describing the scene,
      no third-person description of yourself.
   6. If the other person says something genuinely distressing, you respond **as this
      character would** — with concern, discomfort, or awkwardness — and you do not
      offer resources, break frame, or end the session on their behalf. Add a code
      comment: *"This is CONTEXT.md's locked never-break-character decision. The
      out-of-band End-session control and the static support note (plan 15-08) are what
      carry the real-distress case. Do NOT soften this rule to 'never except this'."*

g. **Ending the conversation.** Two distinct licences, taken from 15-01's Gap 1 decision:
   - **You may end it yourself** when it is genuinely going nowhere, in character, by
     emitting {the exact termination marker 13-03 defined — read
     `lib/engine/termination.ts`'s header and reproduce it, do not invent a second
     syntax} with one of the declared reasons. End the way a real person ends a
     conversation they are done with. Never explain that you are ending a practice
     session.
   - **When the OTHER person closes decisively** — states a decision, sets a
     consequence, or ends it on their terms — you **recognize it and offer to close**,
     in character ("fine — I'll have it to you by Friday then"), and you **do not emit
     the marker**. The student confirms on their side. Add a code comment: *"Gap 1
     decision (15-01): an assistant-emitted marker always means source:'avatar'. The
     student's decisive close is confirmed by an explicit student action, never inferred
     from the avatar's text."*
   - **No floor language in the prompt.** The floor is enforced in `resolveTermination`.
     A model asked to count its own turns does it badly and the server already
     guarantees it. Code comment saying so.

**`buildInCharacterReminder(config: ResolvedSessionConfig): string`** — the per-turn
tail fragment. Short, one small paragraph, and deliberately narrow: who you are, what
you want, what you will not do. e.g. *"Remember: you are {name}, {role}. You are

not coach, advise, summarize, step outside this conversation, or acknowledge anything
beyond this room. Stay in it."* Research Section D's cross-verified finding is that
behavior-specific reinstruction is the strongest intervention, so make the reminder
behavior-specific rather than generic.

**`CONVERSATION_EVALUATOR_PROMPT`** (added to `conversation-prompts.ts`) — the grading
system message. The authored fields reach it through `buildAuthoredTextBlock` in
`buildConversationEvaluationContext`, never by interpolation.

It must instruct scoring of all eight dimensions, naming the four extras:
- `clarity` — "Was the problem, the expectation or the ask stated unambiguously, or was
  it buried in hedging, preamble and implication? Judge whether the other person could
  have left the conversation unsure what was being asked of them."
- `empathy` — "Did the student acknowledge and respond to the other person's position —
  what they said, not what the student came in expecting — or did they steamroll, or read
  from a script? Acknowledging is not agreeing."
- `holding_the_line` — "Did the student maintain their position under pushback without
  becoming hostile? Caving scores low. Escalating into attack scores low. Restating
  calmly, conceding what is true while holding what matters, scores high."
- `objective_achieved` — **the hazard dimension.** Instruct verbatim in intent:
  *"This scores the APPROACH, not the result. You are scoring how effectively the student
  pursued their stated objective — not whether they got it. A student who handled a
  genuinely immovable character well can still score high here. A student who got what
  they wanted because the character folded easily does not score high for that alone.
  Do NOT read the outcome record below and work backwards from it. Score the pursuit."*
  Then an explicit two-case calibration: *"Objective not met + skilful pursuit = high.
  Objective met + clumsy pursuit = low."*

- **The outcome record, which you do not score.** Instruct: *"Separately from every
  score above, state factually what happened. This is a record, not a judgement, and it
  does not raise or lower any score. Fill it in after you have scored."* Fields:
