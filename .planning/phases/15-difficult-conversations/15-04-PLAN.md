<non_negotiables>
**From CONTEXT.md — locked:**
1. **Seeded records are CODE, not S3 objects** (research Section E). No seeding script,
   no S3 upload, no migration. They are version-controlled content reviewed by code
   review, they have no `ownerId`, and that absence is what makes ownership unambiguous.
2. **Each record carries all four:** the avatar's hidden position (never shown to the
   student), the shared backstory (shown in the briefing), the student's explicit
   objective (shown in the briefing AND given to the evaluator), and the stakes.
3. **Difficulty is the only tuning dial.** A seeded record declares a default band; the
   student may pick another at setup. Nothing else about a seeded conversation is
   adjustable. **Do not** add industry or role reframing — that is explicitly deferred.
4. **Do NOT ship both sides of a situation.** "You confront" vs "you are confronted" is
   in `<deferred>`. Seven situations, seven records, one side each.
5. **The count is 6-8.** This plan ships seven.
</non_negotiables>

<task type="auto">
  <name>Task 1: Deterministic avatar assignment from the live catalog</name>
  <files>lib/difficult-conversation/avatar-assignment.ts</files>
  <action>
Read `app/api/interview/interviewers/route.ts` first. Its doc comment records the real
constraint: the account-wide avatar catalog is fetched LIVE from HeyGen and filtered to
ACTIVE, non-expired avatars that have a `default_voice`, because the account's key
cannot access HeyGen's separate Platform voice catalog and arbitrary voice ids have not
been validated with LiveAvatar sessions.

**The risk, stated in the file header:** a seeded record that hardcodes an `avatarId` is
exposed to exactly the account-level catalog churn that endpoint exists to filter
against. Every avatar picker in the product filters live; a hardcoded seed would not get
that protection, and would fail at session-start time for a student rather than loudly
at build or verification time.

**`assignSeededAvatar(conversationId: string, preference?: { genderHint?: string }): Promise<{ avatarId: string; voiceId: string }>`:**
- Fetch the live ACTIVE catalog through the same filtering the interviewers route
  already does. **Reuse that filtering** — extract it into a shared helper if it is
  inline in the route, rather than writing a second filter.
- Pick deterministically: sort the filtered catalog by avatar id, then index by a stable
  hash of `conversationId`. The same conversation gets the same avatar across sessions
  as long as the catalog is unchanged, and no conversation's choice is hardcoded.
- If `genderHint` is supplied and any ACTIVE avatar matches it, restrict to that subset
  first and fall back to the full set when it is empty — a hint, never a requirement.
- Always return the avatar's OWN `voice.id` as `voiceId`. Never cross-pair. Add a comment
  citing `CaseAvatar`'s pairing discipline.
- If the catalog is empty or the fetch fails, THROW with a message naming the real
  cause. A seeded conversation that cannot resolve an avatar must fail loudly at
  session start with a diagnosable error, not silently substitute something.
  </action>
  <verify>
`npx tsc --noEmit` clean.
`grep -rn "avatarId: \"" lib/difficult-conversation/` returns nothing — no hardcoded id
anywhere in the module.
Call `assignSeededAvatar` twice for the same id in a throwaway snippet and assert the
same pair comes back; call it for two different ids and record whether they differ.
`grep -n "voice" lib/difficult-conversation/avatar-assignment.ts` shows the voice taken
from the chosen avatar's own record.
  </verify>
  <done>Seeded conversations resolve an avatar from the live ACTIVE catalog, deterministically per conversation, with the avatar's own paired voice, and fail loudly rather than silently when the catalog cannot supply one.</done>

`export const SEEDED_CONVERSATIONS: DifficultConversationRecord[]` — seven records, each
with `ownerId: null`, `published: true`, `lastCheck: null` (seeded text is reviewed by
code review; it does not go through the student pre-publish screen), `avatarId: ""` and
`voiceId: ""` with a comment that these are filled at resolve time by
`assignSeededAvatar` — and a matching `genderHint` where the character's name implies one.

**The brief's four (verbatim situations from `one-on-one-interactions-brief.md`):**

1. `confront-low-performer` — Confronting a low-performing team member.
   Student is their manager. Hidden position: she believes the real problem is being
   given work with no spec and a teammate who misses handoffs, she has been carrying
   two people's load, and she will not accept a written plan unless the manager
   acknowledges the handoff problem first. Shared backstory: two missed deadlines this
   quarter, one prior verbal conversation six weeks ago, strong work in the first half
   of the year. Objective: get a commitment to a specific written improvement plan with
   dates. Stakes: if it escalates she goes to HR about workload; if it goes nowhere the
   team misses the next release.
2. `fire-team-member` — Firing a team member.
   Hidden position: he half-expects it, is frightened about his visa/health insurance,
   and will cycle through bargaining, then anger about never being warned, then asking
   what he tells his family. Shared backstory: a documented three-month plan, two
   missed checkpoints, HR has signed off, last day is in two weeks. Objective: deliver
   the decision clearly, do not negotiate it, and get him through the logistics with
   dignity. Stakes: a muddled delivery leaves him believing it is reversible and
   creates a legal exposure.
3. `ask-for-raise` — Asking a manager for a raise.
   Student is the employee; the avatar is the manager. Hidden position: he agrees she is
   underpaid but his budget was cut, he has a lower number he can get approved today and
   a higher one that needs his own boss, and he will offer the low number first and
   only move if she makes a specific, evidenced case. Shared backstory: she led the
   migration, took on a departed colleague's work, last raise was 22 months ago, review
   cycle is four months away. Objective: a specific number and a date, not "we'll
   revisit it". Stakes: another vague deferral and she starts interviewing.
4. `challenge-grade` — Challenging a professor over an unfair grade.
   Hidden position: she privately knows the rubric row was ambiguous but will not say so
   unprompted, is defensive about being second-guessed, has had three other students
   complain this week, and will regrade only if the student argues from the rubric
   rather than from effort or from their GPA. Shared backstory: the assignment, the
   rubric, the grade, one prior email that went unanswered. Objective: get a concrete
   regrade or a written explanation tied to the rubric. Stakes: the grade decides a
   scholarship threshold.

**The three neighbours (Claude's Discretion — chosen for coverage of a different power
direction or a different kind of pressure, and recorded as such in a comment):**

5. `deliver-bad-news-client` — Telling a client the project has slipped six weeks.
   Student is the account lead; the avatar is the client. Hidden position: he has already
   promised the original date to his own board, is less angry about the slip than about
   finding out late, and will accept a new date only with a credible explanation of
   what changed and what prevents it recurring. Objective: land the new date and keep the
   relationship. Stakes: he has a competitor's proposal on his desk.
   *Covers: outward-facing, no authority over the other person.*
6. `peer-conflict` — A peer who keeps presenting your team's work as his own.
   Neither party has authority over the other. Hidden position: he genuinely does not
   see it that way, believes he did the synthesis that made the work land, and will
   become cold and procedural if accused outright — but will agree to a concrete
   attribution practice if given a way to keep face. Objective: an explicit agreement
   about attribution going forward. Stakes: escalating to a shared manager damages both.
   *Covers: no power differential — holding the line without authority.*
7. `decline-senior-request` — Saying no to a senior leader's request you cannot absorb.
   Hidden position: she has committed the date upward already, does not actually know
   what else your team is carrying, and will respect a clear no with an alternative far
   more than a reluctant yes — but will push twice first. Objective: decline the scope
   or renegotiate it, without damaging the relationship or quietly accepting it. Stakes:
   accepting sinks two committed deliverables.
   *Covers: upward refusal — the inverse of #3's upward ask.*

For every record: `situation` is a paragraph written to the STUDENT in second person and
contains nothing from the hidden position. `sharedBackstory` is facts both sides know.
`hiddenPosition` is written in second person TO THE CHARACTER ("you believe...", "you
will not...") so it reads as character direction in the avatar's prompt. Default
`difficulty: "guarded"` for all seven, with a comment that the middle band is the
default and the student chooses.

Also export `findSeededConversation(id: string): DifficultConversationRecord | undefined`.

Add a file header: *"Seeded conversations are code records with no owner (research
Section E). They are playable by anyone, editable only by code review, and deliberately
have no ownerId so the authoring routes' owner-scoped loaders return null for them —
which is what makes a seeded id un-editable and un-deletable through the API."*
