<non_negotiables>
- **Named, concrete, invented people.** Each record has a real first and last name, an
  employer (an INVENTED company name — never a real company), a seniority, a field, a
  years-in detail and a manner. 16-CONTEXT.md rejects unnamed role archetypes and
  rejects archetypes that invent a name at runtime, because the name must not differ
  run to run.
- **No real people and no real employers.** Do not use a recognisable company name, and
  do not pick a name that reads as a specific public figure. These records need no
  attestation precisely because they are fictional — that stays true only if they
  actually are.
- **The organizing axis is SENIORITY AND FIELD, not difficulty.** 16-CONTEXT.md's
  Deferred Ideas explicitly defers a difficulty ladder. No record may carry a
  `difficulty`, `level`, `hardness` or equivalent field, and no record's persona text
  may describe the person as "easy" or "difficult" to talk to. Varied manner (warm,
  time-pressed, dry, over-busy) is fine and intended; a difficulty SCALE is not.
- **No pinned avatar or voice.** No record may carry `avatarId`, `voice` or
  `previewUrl`. Decision 10: the student picks from the existing Phase 2 catalog
  (`app/api/interview/interviewers/route.ts`), which also avoids the "pinned avatar went
  INACTIVE upstream" failure class.
- **No setting.** 16-CONTEXT.md defers the networking SETTING (conference reception vs.
  coffee chat). A character's persona text must not assume a venue — write it so a
  setting clause could be composed in later by 16-07's prompt assembly without
  rewriting any character.
- `persona` must be a grammatical continuation of "You are playing the role of: " and
  `<= MAX_PERSONA_LENGTH` (600), imported from `lib/interview/customization.ts` — the
  same contract the distiller's output satisfies, so a character and a brought-in person
  are interchangeable downstream.
- Do NOT edit `lib/engine/registry.ts` here. `git diff --stat lib/engine` must be empty.
- `next build` is known-broken on `/about` prerender and is unrelated. Verify with
  `tsc --noEmit` / `eslint` / `next dev`.
</non_negotiables>

  <action>
Read `lib/engine/registry.ts` first and match its house style for a code-record config
module.

Declare:

```ts
export type NetworkingCharacter = {
  /** Stable, permanent id. Appears in InteractionReport.inputSnapshot forever. */
  id: string;
  /** The person's name, fixed in code. */
  displayName: string;
  /** One line for the picker card: role at employer. */
  headline: string;
  /** Picker-card prose: two or three sentences a student reads before choosing. */
  blurb: string;
  /** Organizing axes. NOT a difficulty ladder — see the file header. */
  seniority: "peer" | "senior" | "executive";
  field: string;
  /** Grammatical continuation of "You are playing the role of: ", <= MAX_PERSONA_LENGTH. */
  persona: string;
};
```

Write FIVE records (within 16-CONTEXT.md's four-to-six band), spanning seniority AND
field. Use these slots, and invent the names, employers and prose yourself — this is
Claude's-Discretion copywriting, subject to the spread:

1. **A venture investor** — `seniority: "senior"`, field "venture capital". Partner at
   an invented early-stage fund. Manner: curious, pattern-matching, interrupts to ask
   "so what" questions, decides fast whether a conversation is worth continuing.
2. **A technical recruiter** — `seniority: "senior"`, field "technical recruiting".
   In-house at an invented mid-size software company. Manner: warm and fast, screens for
   fit within two minutes, asks what the student actually wants rather than waiting to be
   asked.
3. **An executive** — `seniority: "executive"`, field one OUTSIDE software (e.g. consumer
   goods, healthcare systems, logistics) so the set is not all tech. VP or above at an
   invented company. Manner: courteous but genuinely short on time, gives two sentences
   and waits, warms considerably if the student is concrete.
4. **A peer** — `seniority: "peer"`, field a specific individual-contributor role two or
   three years out of school. Manner: relaxed, chatty, happy to talk shop, will not
   drive the conversation — the student has to.
5. **An adjacent-field senior contact** — `seniority: "senior"`, a field the student
   likely is NOT in (e.g. a management consultant, a policy or nonprofit director, an
   academic turned practitioner). Manner: thoughtful, asks why the student is talking to
   *them specifically*, which is exactly the question a weak self-introduction cannot
   answer.

Each `persona` must: name the person and their invented employer; state seniority, field
and years in; state the manner concretely in behavioral terms the model can act on ("you
give short answers until the other person says something specific"); and stay inside 600
characters. Do NOT put a venue, a time of day or an event in the persona (the setting is
deferred). Do NOT put evaluation instructions in the persona — that is the evaluator
prompt's job (16-07).

Also export:
- `getNetworkingCharacter(id): NetworkingCharacter | null` — null, never throw, for an
  unknown id (matching `getEngineType`'s posture).
- `listNetworkingCharacters(): NetworkingCharacter[]` — declaration order, which is the
  picker's order.

File header comment must state: the records are CODE, type-checked and reviewable in a
PR, with no migration and no seed script (decision 9); the organizing axis is seniority
and field, and a difficulty ladder is DEFERRED, not forgotten (name
16-CONTEXT.md's Deferred Ideas); no character pins an avatar or voice (decision 10);
every person here is fictional, which is why no attestation applies to this path; and no
persona assumes a setting, so a setting clause can be composed in later without touching
these records.
