<objective>
Stop the pipeline from treating MediaPipe's predicted `visibility` score as an
observation. A landmark whose coordinates fall outside the frame must not count
as visible, no matter how confident the model is that it knows where an
unseen body part would be.

Purpose: 12-09 added a proportional coverage gate and it did not close the
item-7 failure, because it fixed the wrong layer. The gate asked HOW MUCH of the
session a signal was visible for; the defect is that the pipeline was never
measuring visibility at all. A proportional gate over a fabricated signal is
still a gate over a fabricated signal.

Output: landmark gating that means what it says, the diagnostic readings that
should have preceded any gate tuning, a replay assertion built from those real
readings, and a re-run of the sign-off items that remain open.
</objective>

<observed_failure>
Recorded 2026-10-02 at 12-09's Task 3, run by the user against a live session.

**The session:** body essentially entirely off camera, face detected for ~1% of
the session, right arm in shot for part of it.

**What the report said:**
  - Posture drift: **"Shifted from the opening posture"**
  - Measured from: **Shoulder line and Head position and Torso lean and Torso openness**
  - Moments: `0:01-4:20 [Camera] Off camera`, then eight body-language rows —
    "Posture shifted from the start of the session" at `0:27-2:56` and
    `3:07-3:49`, "A lot of hand movement" x3, "Very still delivery" x3
  - Gesturing: Well judged; Hands near face: Frequent

At 12-08 the same test produced a false CLEAN BILL ("Held steady"). It now
produces a false FINDING with timecodes. That is a regression in character: the
student is told their posture shifted during a session nothing could see.

**The contradiction that identifies the cause.** `forward_head` visibility
requires `NOSE` AND an ear (`visual-capture.worker.ts:543-546`). The 12-09 gate
requires that to hold for >=25% of expected pose samples. The face was detected
~1% of the session. Both cannot be true of a real body.

**Root cause.** `isVisible` (`visual-capture.worker.ts:271`) tests exactly one
thing:

```ts
return (landmarks[index]?.visibility ?? 0) >= LANDMARK_VISIBILITY_FLOOR;
```

MediaPipe's `visibility` is a model-PREDICTED probability, not an observation.
Having locked onto a partial body — the right arm — the model emits a full
33-landmark skeleton and assigns confident visibility to shoulders, nose and
ears outside the frame entirely. Nothing checks whether a landmark's coordinates
fall within the image. The extrapolated skeleton cleared the visibility floor on
most ticks, so it also cleared the new ratio gate.

**What 12-09 got right and keeps.** The renderer refusal
(`visualBodyLanguageBands` gating the row on `posture_signals_measured`), the
episode filter (`filterUnreadableBodyLanguageEpisodes`), and the section-10b
replay assertions are all correct and stay. They sit downstream of a producer
that lies, and become effective once this plan fixes the producer. Do NOT revert
them. The coverage ratio also stays — it is a reasonable secondary condition
once the primary signal is honest.
</observed_failure>

<constraint_do_not_overcorrect>
REQ-51 still pulls the other way and still must not be broken:

> "A partially visible body is scored on the landmarks that ARE available rather
> than skipped, and every posture comment states which were measured."

A student with shoulders in frame and legs out of frame must still be scored on
the shoulder line. The goal is that `visible.*` means **observed in frame**,
not **the model is confident it could guess this**. Landmarks genuinely inside
the frame keep counting exactly as they do today.

Per `12-CONTEXT.md`'s calibration rule, where the two sides conflict the
unreadable side wins.
</constraint_do_not_overcorrect>
